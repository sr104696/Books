# Question Bank Cheat Sheet

Do not memorize hundreds of answers. Identify the evaluator’s intent, use one structure, and land a pillar.

| Meta-category | What they are really testing | Structure | Example response skeleton |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Identity & trajectory** (“Tell me about yourself”) | Coherence and fit | Pitch Formula A/B | “I help **[who]** achieve **[outcome]**. Recently I **[proof]**. I am here because **[need]**.” |
| 2. **Motivation & fit** (“Why this role/company?”) | Intent and research | P-O-V | “I am drawn to **[specific problem]**. My **[proof]** makes it a fit. I want to help with **[outcome]**.” |
| 3. **Achievement & strengths** | Repeatable impact | H-E-A-R | “A strength I rely on is **[verb]**. In **[context]**, I **[actions]**, producing **[result]**.” |
| 4. **Behavior under pressure** (“Tell me about a challenge/conflict”) | Judgment, ownership, collaboration | H-E-A-R | “The stakes were **[x]**. I aligned **[people]**, chose **[actions]**, and **[result/learning]**.” |
| 5. **Failure, weakness & feedback** | Self-awareness and change | H-E-A-R or G-A-P | “I initially **[bounded mistake]**. I owned it by **[action]**; since then **[changed behavior/proof]**.” |
| 6. **Missing experience / transition** | Hiring risk and learning speed | G-A-P | “I have not **[gap]**; however, I solved **[adjacent problem]** with **[result]**. My ramp is **[plan]**.” |
| 7. **Scenario, technical & prioritization** | Thinking, not recall alone | C-A-R-R | “I would first define **[success]**, then **[three steps]**, check **[risk]**, and measure **[signal]**.” |
| 8. **Future, close & candidate questions** | Commitment, judgment, mutual fit | P-O-V + bridge | “In the first 90 days I would learn **[x]**, validate **[y]**, and deliver **[z]**. May I ask **[diagnostic question]**?” |

## One example: no direct experience
**Question:** “Have you managed a budget this large?”  
**Answer:** “I have not owned a budget at that scale. In my last role, I did own the prioritization that affected vendor spend: I compared options, surfaced trade-offs, and helped the team avoid an unnecessary renewal. The transferable part is disciplined decision-making with stakeholders. I would learn your approval rules and historical spend quickly, then start by producing a transparent options analysis before making recommendations.”

## Diagnostic questions to ask them
- “What outcome would make the first six months a clear success?”
- “Where does this work most often get stuck: priorities, process, data, or alignment?”
- “What distinguishes people who become effective quickly on this team?”
