# Universal Answer Superstructures

Use one structure, then stop. A strong answer is usually 45–120 seconds: headline, proof, relevance.

## 1. H-E-A-R: universal behavioral answer
**Headline** — answer the question in one sentence.  
**Environment** — situation, stakes, and your role.  
**Action** — 2–3 choices you personally made.  
**Result + relevance** — outcome, learning, and link to this role.

> “Yes—my approach is to **[headline]**. In **[environment]**, the challenge was **[stakes]**. I **[action 1]** and **[action 2]**. That led to **[result]**. It is relevant here because **[JD need]**.”

**Use for:** Tell me about a time, strengths, conflict, leadership, failure, ambiguity.

## 2. C-A-R-R: unknown technical, judgment, or situational question
**Clarify** the goal, constraints, and success measure.  
**Approach** with a numbered plan.  
**Risk-check** assumptions, stakeholders, and trade-offs.  
**Result** state how you would measure/adjust.

> “Before choosing an answer, I would clarify **[goal/constraint]**. My first steps would be **[1]**, **[2]**, and **[3]**. I would check **[risk/trade-off]** with **[stakeholder/data]**. I would know it is working when **[measure]**; if not, I would **[adjustment]**.”

**Use for:** “What would you do if…?”, unfamiliar tools, cases, prioritization.

## 3. G-A-P: candidly handle a missing qualification
**Gap** — name it without apology or evasion.  
**Adjacent proof** — similar problem/capability and result.  
**Plan** — exact learning/validation steps and early contribution.

> “I have not yet **[gap]**. The closest relevant experience is **[adjacent proof]**, where I **[action/result]**. To close the domain piece, I would **[plan]**; meanwhile, I could contribute immediately through **[transferable deliverable]**.”

**Use for:** lack of experience, career transition, tool/domain gap, “Why should we hire you?”

## 4. P-O-V: values, motivation, and opinion questions
**Position** — your principle or preference.  
**One example** — brief evidence it guides behavior.  
**Value here** — connect it to the team’s actual work.

> “My view is **[position]** because **[reason]**. For example, **[brief proof]**. In this role that matters because **[relevance]**.”

**Use for:** Why this company, management style, culture, success/failure, unpopular opinion.

## Answer-control rules
- If the question is vague: clarify before performing. “When you say **[term]**, are you most interested in **[A]** or **[B]**?”
- If you need a moment: “Let me choose the most relevant example.”
- If no direct story exists: use C-A-R-R, not a fabricated story.
- Close with a bridge: “The part most applicable here is **[pillar]**.”
