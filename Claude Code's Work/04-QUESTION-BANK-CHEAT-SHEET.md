# Question-Bank Cheat Sheet — 8 Meta-Categories

Every specific interview question you'll encounter is a variant of one of these 8. Don't memorize hundreds of questions — recognize the category, apply its structure (from file 03), and adapt.

---

### 1. THE OPENER
*"Tell me about yourself" · "Walk me through your background" · "What makes you unique" · "Why should we hire you" · "Rate yourself 1-10"*

**Structure:** Your 60-Second Pitch (file 01) — these are all the same question in different clothes. Never treat as small talk to improvise; it's typically the first real question.

**Example:** *"I got my start [concrete hook]. For the past six years I've worked in supply-chain ops, most recently leading a re-platforming project that cut fulfillment time by 30%. What's mattered most to me throughout is [Fit pillar] — which is why I've stayed hands-on with frontline teams even as I've moved up. Outside work I coach a youth robotics team, which honestly sharpened my project-management instincts more than any course did. That's part of why [Company]'s focus on [researched fact] stood out to me. What's the biggest priority for whoever steps into this role?"*

---

### 2. PROOF / BEHAVIORAL
*"Tell me about a time you..." · "Give an example of..." · "Describe your greatest accomplishment" · "3 strengths"*

**Structure:** The Story (Structure 1, file 03) — Situation → [Obstacles] → Action → Result.

**Example** ("time you solved a problem under pressure"): *"Two weeks before a client launch, our lead vendor dropped out. I re-sourced two backup vendors within 48 hours, personally validated their samples, and we shipped on the original date — the client renewed for triple the contract value the following quarter."*

---

### 3. NEGATIVE / WEAKNESS
*"Greatest weakness" · "Biggest failure" · "Worst boss" · "Tell me about a mistake" · "What would your boss say you need to improve"*

**Structure:** The Pivot (Structure 2, file 03) — short honest negative, sandwiched, most airtime on the fix. All of these are functionally the *same* underlying question asked from different angles to catch inconsistency — prepare **one** consistent story and reuse its substance regardless of phrasing.

**Example:** *"I used to over-own things — I'd take on tasks that should've been delegated because I didn't trust the handoff. On a project last year I caught myself doing it again, and forced myself to write out a delegation plan before kickoff instead of after. It's now just part of how I start every project."*

---

### 4. MOTIVATION / FIT
*"Why us" · "Why this role" · "Where do you see yourself in 5 years" · "Ideal work environment" · "What motivates you"*

**Structure:** Rubric-Map (Structure 3) anchored on your Fit pillar, backed by real company research. Fry's rule: a vague or absent answer here is close to disqualifying — this is the one category where "I didn't have time to prepare" is not survivable.

**Example** ("why us"): *"Three things drew me here: [specific product/mission fact], the way [specific team practice you learned in research] matches how I like to work, and honestly, talking to [name/someone] about [specific detail] confirmed it wasn't just a good pitch on the website."*

---

### 5. OBJECTION / UNDERQUALIFIED / HYPOTHETICAL
*"You don't have X years of experience" · "How would you handle..." · Case/brainteaser questions · Any question probing a visible gap*

**Structure:** The Pivot with full ARTS (Structure 2) for stated objections; for hypotheticals, answer the scenario **then proactively bridge to a real example**: *"That's how I'd approach it — would it help to hear about a similar situation I actually faced?"* (This is the single sharpest move in the whole system for closing the credibility gap hypotheticals leave open.)

For genuine gaps: name it yourself before it becomes a silent disqualifier, then pivot to a "fast learner" proof story — a specific past example of self-teaching a skill quickly.

**Example** ("you haven't managed a team before"): *"That's fair — I haven't had the title yet. What I have done is informally led three cross-functional launches where I was setting the timeline and unblocking other people's work, which is most of what the job actually is day to day. I'd also add — I'm someone who ramps fast; when I moved into [X] I was fully independent within six weeks. Would it help if I walked through that?"*

---

### 6. ILLEGAL / PERSONAL / INVASIVE
*Age, marital/family status, religion, national origin, disability, health, arrest record*

**Structure:** The 3-rung ladder in file 03/06 — answer the underlying business concern (reliability, travel, commitment) without confirming or denying the protected fact; escalate to flagging it as off-topic only if it persists.

**Example** (asked about young kids at home / travel concerns): *"I sense you might be wondering about my flexibility to travel — I want to assure you that's not an issue; I traveled 40% of the time in my last role and it worked well."*

---

### 7. SALARY / LOGISTICS / CLOSING
*"Salary expectations" · "When can you start" · "Do you have other offers" · "Any reservations about hiring me"*

**Structure:** Never volunteer a number first (see file 06 for the exact deflection ladder). For logistics questions, treat every one as still evaluative — hedging on start date/relocation reads as low interest even late in the process. For "any reservations," this is your invitation to run ARTS live.

**Example** (salary pressed early): *"I'd rather make sure we're the right match first — once we agree on that, I'm confident we can land on something fair for both sides. What range did you have budgeted for this role?"*

---

### 8. CURVEBALL / STRESS / OFF-THE-WALL
*"Rate this interview" · Rudeness/silence/interruptions · "What's the last book you read" · Puzzle questions ("golf balls in a 747")*

**Structure:** Recognize it as a deliberate test of composure and process, not content. For stress tactics: stay neutral, pause before answering, never match hostility (tell: friendliness often returns abruptly at the end if it was a manufactured test). For puzzles: think out loud, question assumptions, don't rush — the reasoning is what's scored, not the final number. For personal/small-talk questions: safe, mainstream, business-adjacent answers only — these are being screened from the first second of contact, there's no "off the clock" moment.

**Example** (puzzle question): *"Let me think through this out loud — first I'd want to clarify whether we mean a 747-400 or -800, since that changes the cargo volume assumption significantly... [reasoning] ...so roughly X, landing within an order of magnitude either way."*
