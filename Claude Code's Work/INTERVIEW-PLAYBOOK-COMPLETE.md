# Interview Playbook — README

This playbook is distilled from six books in this repo: Fry, *101 Great Answers*; LaCivita, *Interview Intervention*; Kelley, *Get That Job*; Kador, *301 Best Questions to Ask*; Klaus, *Brag!*; and Bolles, *What Color Is Your Parachute?* (2011). Two other source files (`101 Great Questions.pdf` and Dalton's *2-Hour Job Search*) were empty (0 bytes) and contributed nothing.

## The whole system in 4 lines

1. **3 pillars:** three messages you get across no matter what you're asked.
   - **PROVE:** I can do it.
   - **FIT:** I'll mesh here.
   - **EDGE:** I'm not interchangeable.
2. **4 shapes:** every answer is a **STORY**, **PIVOT**, **MAP** or **CLOSE**.
3. **8 question types:** every question fits one of eight. Each has a hidden fear and a default shape.
4. **The loop:** for any question, decode it, pick a shape, anchor it to a pillar, and end on a forward note.

Built for when you're underqualified: name the gap first, prove you learn fast, ask for their hiring criteria, and ask what objections they have.

# 01 · The 60-Second Pitch

"Tell me about yourself," "walk me through your background," "why should we hire you" and "what makes you unique" are the same question. It usually comes first. Never improvise it, and never just recite your history in order.

**Length:** about 150–250 words, or 60–90 seconds. Hard cap 2 minutes; the best-received answers stay under that. Speak from bullet points, not a memorized script, and practice out loud until it sounds fresh.

## The formula (7 beats)

| Beat | Content | Pillar |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Hook | One concrete line about how you started or a specific moment, not a job title | EDGE |
| 2 Summary | Years, field, current or last role. Mention years only if they meet what's asked. | PROVE |
| 3 Throughline | What consistently drives you, or what you're known for | FIT |
| 4 Proof | 1–2 results with numbers (exact figures beat adjectives) | PROVE |
| 5 Edge | What others with your title lack (passes the REV filter) | EDGE |
| 6 Why here | One researched, specific fact tied to your interests | FIT |
| 7 Handback | End with a question; never trail off | control |

A human detail (a hobby, an unusual background fact) goes in beat 1 or 5. Never mention age, kids, religion or politics.

## Fill in the blanks

> "[HOOK]. For [X years] I've [core function] in [field], most recently as [role] at [company]. What people count on me for is [throughline], and that's shown up as [result with number]. What I'd add that's hard to find is [edge]. That's why [company]'s [specific researched thing] stood out — [why it matters to me]. [Question, e.g. 'What's the biggest priority for whoever fills this role?']"

## Variants (same parts, rearranged)

- **20 seconds:** beats 2, 5 and 7.
- **Underqualified:** open with beat 3 (what you do that matters). Give beat 4 proof from related work, then name the gap and how fast you learn before beat 7.
- **Career changer:** lead with the transferable skill, phrased as verb + object + manner ("untangling messy vendor data fast"), not your old title.
- **Sharper handback:** "How are you addressing [trend you researched]?" This steers toward their problems.

## Checks
- Could any other candidate say the same thing? Then sharpen your edge.
- Does it touch on anything they care about? If not, tie it to the job description.
- Are at least 3 claims backed by a number or a story? If not, cut the unsupported claims.

# 02 · The Three Pillars

Every interviewer is quietly scoring three things. Bolles's "5 real questions," Kelley's Competence/Compatibility/Chemistry and the REV filter all come down to these:

| Pillar | Their unspoken question | Your message |
|---|---|---|
| **PROVE** | Can they do the job? | Evidence of results in the core function |
| **FIT** | Will they mesh with us, and do they want *this* job? | Values, work style, real reasons for choosing this company |
| **EDGE** | Why them over 19 (or 900) others? | A trait or story others can't claim |

Repeat these three messages throughout the interview. Interviewers remember a few short phrases, not your whole history, so plant the same three consistently.

## Choose them with the REV filter
List about 10 possible messages from your resume, performance reviews and the questions in 05. Keep only the ones that pass all three tests:
- **Relevant:** matches what *this* job description and employer need. Use their wording.
- **Exceptional:** not a generic claim. "Hard worker," "team player" and "integrity" fail unless you can show they're unusual.
- **Verifiable:** backed by a number, a story or a quote from someone else.

Write each pillar as one sentence you can say in 10 seconds, and note its proof.

| Pillar | One-sentence message | Proof (story ID from 05, number or quote) |
|---|---|---|
| PROVE | | |
| FIT | | |
| EDGE | | |

## How to phrase skills
Never state a bare skill. Use verb + object + manner: "negotiating vendor contracts under tight deadlines." Skills support PROVE. Traits (*how* you work) support EDGE.

## Get ahead of their fears
Interviewers watch for signs that you:
- can't do the job
- are unreliable
- will leave soon
- will be slow to ramp up
- won't get along with people
- will do the minimum
- will need constant direction
- have a character flaw
- will embarrass whoever hired you
- will be an expensive mistake

Each pillar story should quietly answer at least one of these fears. Map yours:

| Likely fear about you (given your gaps) | Pillar or story that answers it |
|---|---|
| | |

## For each job
Re-tune your pillars to each job description. Your stories stay the same; you change which pillar leads. If one pillar is thin, tell your LLM or coach before the interview and prepare the gap script (PIVOT, in 03) for it.

# 03 · Universal Answer Superstructures

Interview questions come in two kinds: **"What did you do?"** (past, proof) and **"What would you do?"** (hypothetical). Every one of them can be answered with one of four shapes.

## THE LOOP (run it on every question, especially unprepared ones)

1. **Decode.** Take a 2–5 second pause, or restate the question if it is long. Ask yourself two things: which fear sits behind it (see 04), and is it a *did* or a *would* question?
2. **Pick a shape.** STORY, PIVOT, MAP or CLOSE.
3. **Anchor.** Land on one pillar: PROVE, FIT or EDGE.
4. **Land.** End in one of four ways: on the result, with a callback to the question, with a bridge to their situation ("Is that similar to what you face here?"), or with a question. Never trail off.

Length: under 2 minutes, and often 20–40 seconds is enough. If you run long, say "Bottom line: …" and stop. You can also offer more: "Want more detail on any part?"

---

## S1 · STORY — for proof questions
*Use for: "Tell me about a time," strengths, accomplishments.*

**S**ituation → **O**bstacle (optional) → **A**ction → **R**esult
- **Situation:** one line of context, naming the pain (lost time, money or opportunity) without blaming anyone.
- **Obstacle:** include it only if you were the one who overcame it. Leave it out rather than invent one.
- **Action:** say "I," not "we." Give enough detail to be believable.
- **Result:** give a number. Add evidence that it lasted, or a quote from a boss or client if you have one. The result can also go first: "Let me tell you how I cut onboarding time in half…"

For a hypothetical ("would") question, answer it and then add: *"Would it help to hear about a time I actually handled something like that?"* Most interviewers never ask the "did" follow-up, so offer the real proof yourself.

## S2 · PIVOT — for negative, objection or hostile questions
*Use for: weakness, failure, "why did you leave," gaps, being underqualified, invasive questions.*

**Own it → Keep it short → Fix → Forward**
1. **Own it.** State the real issue plainly. Don't get defensive and don't dodge.
2. **Keep it short.** The negative should take about one fifth of the answer. Use "an area I've developed," not "my problem."
3. **Fix.** Spend most of the answer here: what you changed, learned or built, and how the flip side of the trait helps you.
4. **Forward.** Finish on the strength or the result, never on the flaw.

When they raise a **stated objection**, use ARTS:
- **Acknowledge:** "That's a fair concern."
- **Redirect:** "What would you need to see to feel confident there?"
- **Test:** "If I could show you X, would that help?"
- **Support:** give a specific story.

For an **invasive or illegal question**, answer the business concern behind it (reliability, travel, commitment) without confirming or denying the personal fact. The exact phrases are in 06.

## S3 · MAP — for vague or unprepared questions
*Use for: "What value do you bring," "What motivates you," "Why should we hire you," and anything you didn't see coming.*

**Clarify (once) → Match to a pillar → Prove → Tie back**
1. **Clarify.** "Happy to — which part of the role matters most to you, so I focus there?" Ask this only once; more than once looks evasive.
2. **Match.** Pick the pillar that fits their answer. Don't invent a new message.
3. **Prove.** Give a 15-second STORY.
4. **Tie back.** "…which is exactly the [thing they just named]."

## S4 · CLOSE — for the last 5 minutes and "Any questions?"
**Confirm → Assure → Ask**
1. **Confirm:** "So you need someone who can [X] and [Y]. Did I get that right?"
2. **Assure:** match your three pillars to those needs, in one sentence each.
3. **Ask:** "I'm excited about this. Is there anything that gives you pause? … What's the next step?"
Then run the follow-up sequence in 06.

# 04 · Question Bank — 8 Types

Don't memorize answers to hundreds of questions. Learn to recognize these eight types. Behind every question is a fear about you; answer that fear.

| # | Type | Sample wordings | Hidden fear | Shape | Pillar |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Opener | "Tell me about yourself," "Walk me through your background," "Why hire you?" | "Are they generic?" | Pitch (01) | All |
| Q2 | Proof | "Tell me about a time…," "Greatest accomplishment," "3 strengths" | "Are their claims real?" | STORY | PROVE |
| Q3 | Negative | "Greatest weakness," "Biggest failure," "A mistake you made," "Worst boss" | "Are they self-aware, or a problem?" | PIVOT | PROVE |
| Q4 | Motivation & fit | "Why us?," "Why this role?," "Where in 5 years?," "Ideal environment?" | "Will they stay and engage?" | MAP | FIT |
| Q5 | Gap & hypothetical | "You lack X," "How would you handle…," case or puzzle questions | "Can they actually do it?" | PIVOT/ARTS, or answer then STORY | PROVE |
| Q6 | History | "Why are you leaving?," "Employment gap?," "Were you fired?," "Job-hopping?" | "Will it happen again here?" | PIVOT | FIT |
| Q7 | Money & logistics | "Salary expectations?," "Start date?," "Other offers?," "Relocate?" | "Can we afford them, and are they serious?" | Deflect (06) | FIT |
| Q8 | Curveball & stress | "Rate this interview," a hostile tone, silence, "Last book you read?," "Superpower?" | "How do they act under pressure?" | Calm, then MAP | EDGE |

Invasive or illegal questions (about age, family, religion, health or origin) fall under Q6 or Q7. Use the ladder in 06.

---

## One rule and one example per type

**Q1 Opener.** Use the 7-beat pitch, ending with a question back to them.
> "…That's why your push into mid-market caught my eye. What's the biggest priority for this hire in the first six months?"

**Q2 Proof.** Use STORY, with a number in the result. Choose the story that fits *this* job.
> "Our vendor dropped out two weeks before launch. I lined up two backups in 48 hours, tested their samples myself, and we shipped on the original date. The client tripled its contract the next quarter."

**Q3 Negative.** Prepare one consistent story. Interviewers ask this several ways to catch inconsistency. Pick a real weakness that is either already fixed, the flip side of a strength, or the obvious gap they can already see. Avoid clichés like "perfectionist."
> "I used to hold onto work I should have delegated. Now I write a delegation plan at kickoff, and on my last project that freed me to run the client side and we finished early."

**Q4 Motivation & fit.** Give researched specifics: the work itself and the mission first, culture second. Never lead with money or perks. A vague answer here can rule you out.
> "Three reasons: [a product or mission fact], [a team practice that matches how I work], and [something an employee told me]."

**Q5 Gap & hypothetical.** Name the gap before they do. Offer proof from adjacent experience, then show how fast you learn. For a hypothetical, answer it, then offer a real story. For puzzles, think out loud and state your assumptions.
> "I haven't held the manager title. I have led three cross-functional launches, setting timelines and clearing blockers, which is most of the job. When I moved into analytics I was working on my own within six weeks."

**Q6 History.** Use PIVOT. Give only reasons you couldn't control or growth reasons. Never criticize a past employer. If you were laid off, say so plainly. If you were fired, give one calm sentence and move to what came after. For a gap, describe what you did during it.
> "The company restructured and my role was eliminated. Since then I've earned [certification] and consulted for [client], which is how I got deep into [skill they need]."

**Q7 Money & logistics.** Never name a number first. Use the salary ladder in 06. Hesitating about logistics reads as low interest, so answer firmly.
> "I'd like to make sure we're the right match first. What range did you budget for the role?"

**Q8 Curveball & stress.** It's a test of how you think and how steady you are. Stay neutral, pause, and turn the answer toward the job. Small talk counts too: you are being assessed from the first hello.
> "Superpower? Fast pattern-spotting in messy data, which is basically what your analyst role is asking for."

## "Do you have questions for us?" (always ask at least 4)

Ask about the company before the role, and the role before the boss, so you don't come across as self-focused. Don't ask anything a 2-minute search would answer. Don't ask "what about me" questions (perks, hours) before an offer.

| Purpose | Question |
|---|---|
| Get their criteria (ask early if you can) | "By what criteria will you select the person for this job?" |
| Learn their pain | "What's the most urgent problem the person you hire needs to solve?" |
| Define success | "What does success look like at 90 days and at one year?" |
| Learn priorities | "Roughly what percentage of time goes to each responsibility?" |
| Learn the boss | "How would your team describe your management style?" |
| Get candor | "When your best people leave, where do they go?" |
| Draw out objections | "Is there anything about my background that gives you pause?" |
| Open the door | "What question should I have asked but didn't?" |

After each answer, respond to what they said before moving to your next question.

# 05 · Story Bank

Six stories cover nearly every interview. Most stories prove several things, so tag each one by pillar and skill and you can pull it up fast for a question you didn't expect. In a multi-round process, keep a log of which story you told to whom. Interviewers compare notes.

## 1. Mine your material (10 minutes, in any order)
1. Which 3 results are you proudest of? Include the numbers.
2. Name a hard problem you solved that others couldn't or wouldn't.
3. Name a mistake or failure, and what you changed afterward.
4. When did you learn something new fast and deliver with it?
5. Describe a conflict or disagreement you resolved, and how.
6. When did you lead or influence without having the authority?
7. What has someone praised you for without being asked? Write down their exact words.
8. What unusual personal fact or background would make someone remember you?
9. What 3 traits would colleagues name in you? Ask 2–3 of them. Others often see strengths you discount.

## 2. Six slots to fill
| ID | Slot | Typical questions it answers |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Biggest win (numbers) | Accomplishment, strengths, why hire you |
| S2 | Hard problem / ambiguity | Problem-solving, initiative, pressure |
| S3 | Failure → fix | Weakness, mistake, what you'd do differently |
| S4 | Fast learner | Any gap or "you lack X" objection |
| S5 | People: conflict / influence | Teamwork, a difficult person, leadership |
| S6 | Signature / human | Your EDGE; what makes you memorable |

## 3. Story card (copy six times)
```
ID / TITLE:
PILLAR(S): PROVE / FIT / EDGE        SKILLS (verb+object+manner):
SITUATION (1 line, the pain, no blame):
OBSTACLE (optional, only if you overcame it):
ACTION (I, not we; 2–3 key moves):
RESULT (number + lasting evidence + quote if you have one):
15-SEC VERSION (one sentence):
FEAR IT DISARMS (from 02):
USED WITH (interviewer / round):
```

## 4. Quality check before a story goes in the bank
- [ ] I'm the subject of the action verbs ("I built," not "it happened" or "we did").
- [ ] The result has a number, or a concrete before-and-after.
- [ ] It works in 15 seconds and in 90 seconds.
- [ ] It answers *why this listener should care* (tie it to the JD).
- [ ] It's true and checkable. No inflation, and teammates get credit for their part while you own yours.

## 5. Underqualified? Extend the timeline backward
If you look short on paper, don't inflate. Find the early, concrete origin of the skill (a side project, a family business, a volunteer role, a course you applied right away). That shows years of real interest and competence the title doesn't.

# 06 · Control & Bridging Tactics

These are phrases to use word for word or adapt, grouped by when they come up in the interview. Most interviewers have little training and are improvising, which leaves room for you to guide the conversation politely.

## Opening: set the agenda
- **Ask for their criteria** (the highest-leverage move when you're underqualified, best used with the actual decision-maker): *"May I ask a question before we start? By what criteria will you select the person for this job?"* Note each criterion and ask "What else?" Then spend the interview showing how you meet each one.
- **Hold your questions:** *"I have plenty of questions, but I'd like to respect your process. Happy to save them for later."*
- **Find common ground early:** mention a shared connection, school or interest. Interviewers who like you read ambiguous points in your favor.

## Mid-interview: steer the conversation
| Situation | Phrase |
|---|---|
| A key point hasn't come up | *"Speaking of [X], may I tell you about…"* / *"We haven't touched on [X], and I think it's relevant…"* |
| Hypothetical question | *"…that's how I'd approach it. Would a real example help?"* |
| Vague question | *"Happy to. Which part matters most to you?"* (ask once only) |
| No-win question ("What do you like least about this job?") | Answer with a clarifying question rather than a risky claim. |
| You're rambling | *"Bottom line: …"* then stop. |
| Interviewer heading toward a weak spot | Answer briefly, then ask a substantive question on another topic. |
| Predictable question | Answer, then: *"…and I'd love to hear how the team handles that here."* |
| "Anything else I should know?" | Use it to tell a strong story you haven't used yet. |

## Objections and gaps
- **Name the gap before they do:** *"I'll be direct: I haven't held the title [X]. Here's the adjacent work I've done… and here's how fast I ramp up: [S4]."*
- **ARTS, spoken:** *"That's a fair concern. What prompted it? … If I could show you [X], would that help? Here's an example…"*
- **Draw out silent objections:** *"Is there anything about my fit that gives you pause, that I could address now?"* Objections fall into three kinds:
  - **Misunderstanding:** clarify it.
  - **Blind spot:** share the experience they didn't ask about.
  - **Real gap:** offer a specific plan to close it.
- **Never say** "I've never…" or "I can't…". Say *"I haven't done exactly that, but I have [adjacent]."*

## Invasive or illegal questions (try in this order)
1. **Address the concern behind it:** *"If you're wondering about travel, I traveled 40% of the time in my last role with no problem."*
2. **Answer and neutralize:** *"Yes, [fact]. And [concern] isn't an issue, because [specifics]."*
3. **Redirect gently:** *"I'm not sure that bears on the role, but what I can tell you is…"* If it keeps happening, treat that as information about the employer.

## Salary: whoever names a number first usually loses
1. *"Until we both know I'm the right fit, a number feels premature. What range did you budget?"*
2. *"Glad to discuss. Could you first walk me through the full scope, so I'm comparing like with like?"*
3. Last resort, a researched range: *"For roles like this, I'm seeing $X–$Y."*
- If asked about past pay: *"This role's scope is different, so let's price this job."*
- If asked about other offers: name categories, not company names: *"a couple of later-stage teams."*

## Close: get a clear next step
1. **Confirm, assure, ask:** *"You need [X] and [Y]. Is that right? … That's exactly what I've done at [proof]. I'd really like this role. What's the next step?"*
2. Then ask each of these:
   - *"When can I expect to hear from you?"*
   - *"What's the latest date I should expect to hear?"*
   - *"May I follow up after that date?"*
3. Bolder options, if the mood allows:
   - *"Is there a fit here?"*
   - *"On a scale of 1 to 10, how do I compare, and what would make it a 10?"*
4. If the answer is no: *"Who else should I talk to?"*

## After the interview
- **Thank-you note the same day:** thanks, then 2–3 lines on your fit tied to something specific they said, then interest. Use it to correct any wrong impression you left.
- **After a rejection:** *"I appreciate you letting me know. Would you share one thing I could do better? I won't argue, and I won't contact you again for a year."*

# 08 · One-Pager (the 1-page tier)

*Need more detail? Run Prompt A in file 07 with LENGTH set to 3, 5 or 10 pages. Each tier includes the one below it.*

**THE LOOP:** Decode the fear → Pick a shape → Anchor to a pillar → Land forward. Keep it under 2 minutes.

**MY PILLARS**
- PROVE: ______________________ (proof: ____)
- FIT: ________________________ (proof: ____)
- EDGE: _______________________ (proof: ____)

**PITCH (7 beats):** Hook → Summary → Throughline → Proof # → Edge → Why here → Question back

**4 SHAPES**
| Shape | Steps | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| STORY | Situation → (Obstacle) → Action ("I") → Result (#) | Proof questions |
| PIVOT | Own it → Short negative (1/5) → Fix → Forward | Negatives and objections. For a stated objection use ARTS: Acknowledge → Redirect → Test → Support |
| MAP | Clarify once → Match to a pillar → 15-sec story → Tie back | Vague or unprepared questions |
| CLOSE | Confirm their need → Assure (3 pillars) → Ask the next step | Wrap-up |

**8 TYPES → SHAPE**
1. Opener → Pitch
2. Proof → STORY
3. Negative → PIVOT
4. Motivation → MAP + research
5. Gap / hypothetical → name the gap + S4, or answer then offer a story
6. History → PIVOT, no blame
7. Money → deflect
8. Curveball → calm + MAP

**MY 6 STORIES:** S1 Win ____ · S2 Hard problem ____ · S3 Failure→fix ____ · S4 Fast learner ____ · S5 People ____ · S6 Signature ____

**POWER PHRASES**
- "By what criteria will you select the person for this job?"
- "Would a real example help?"
- "Speaking of X, may I tell you about…"
- "Anything that gives you pause that I could address now?"
- "What range did you budget?"
- "When will I hear? What's the latest? May I follow up after that?"

**NEVER**
- Criticize a past employer.
- Name a number first.
- Trail off at the end of an answer.
- Say "I've never…"
- Ask about perks before an offer.
- Treat small talk as off the record.
