# Story Bank Template

Kelley's data point: interviewers in multi-round processes compare notes, and a story gets "used up" once you've told it — so don't bank just 1-2 stories. Aim for **5-6 well-built ones** for a lean system (expand later if you want the full 20-30 Kelley recommends for heavy interview seasons).

## Step 1 — Mine your material (Klaus's "Take 12", adapted)

Answer these non-sequentially — later questions unlock forgotten material for earlier ones:

1. What are 5 things you and others would say are your personality pluses?
2. What are the 10 most interesting things you've done or that have happened to you (career or not)?
3. What accomplishments are you most proud of (current and past jobs)?
4. What new skill did you learn in the last year?
5. What obstacle did you overcome, and what did the mistake behind it teach you?
6. What's a moment someone else (boss, client, coworker) praised you unprompted — and what exactly did they say?

**Crowdsource it:** ask 2-3 people who've worked with you what they'd say your standout trait is. Klaus's finding: there's a real, exploitable gap between self-perception and how others see you — don't rely only on introspection.

**Don't discard the non-professional stuff.** A hobby, an unusual background detail, a personal project — these are often the most memorable thing about you precisely because interviewers have heard the professional facts from a hundred other candidates. Test: *would this detail make someone remember you specifically?*

## Step 2 — Build each story with this template

For each of your 5-6 stories, fill in:

```
TITLE: _______________________________________________

SITUATION (1 sentence — context + the pain point, no blame):
_______________________________________________________

OBSTACLES (optional — only if you skillfully overcame something):
_______________________________________________________

ACTION (what YOU specifically did — watch your "we"):
_______________________________________________________

RESULT (quantified if possible; add a "sound bite" quote if you have one):
_______________________________________________________

15-SECOND VERSION (one sentence per component, for rapid-fire questions):
_______________________________________________________

SKILLS/PILLAR TAGS (which of your Three Pillars does this prove? what
skills — e.g. "negotiation," "leading under ambiguity" — does it demonstrate?):
_______________________________________________________
```

**Skill phrasing rule (Bolles):** don't leave a skill as a bare adjective ("I'm good at analyzing"). Pair it with an object and manner: *"analyzing complex vendor contracts quickly."* Vague skill words are explicitly called out as unconvincing on their own.

## Step 3 — Tag for retrieval

## Step 4 — Stress-test each story

- **Active-voice audit** (Klaus): does the story use "I" as the subject of action verbs, or does it lapse into "it happened" / "we did" / "it was nothing"? The facts don't need to change — only ownership and specificity do. Rewrite any passive story in first person before it goes in the bank.
- **Audience-benefit check** (Klaus's "Bryan the Boston pitch" lesson): does this story surface the fact most relevant to *this specific listener*, or just the most impressive fact in general? A technically perfect story that never answers "why should this person care" underperforms a rougher, more relevant one.
- **Obstacle honesty check** (Kelley): if the obstacle wasn't genuinely yours to overcome (luck, someone else's work), cut it rather than force it.

## Reuse across a multi-round process

Before each round, note which stories you've already told (to whom) so you don't repeat one to someone who's compared notes with an earlier interviewer. This is the actual reason to bank more than 1-2 stories per skill.
