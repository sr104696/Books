# The Three Pillars Framework

Three books independently converge on the same underlying structure for what an interviewer is actually scoring, even when they never say so:

- Bolles (*Parachute*): every question, however phrased, is really one of 5 questions — which collapse into 3 territories.
- Kelley (*Get That Job*): the "Three Cs" — **Competence, Compatibility, Chemistry**.
- Kador (*301 Questions*): interviewers are silently checking a rubric that's almost always some mix of can-do / will-fit / stands-out.

**Merged into one framework — your Three Pillars:**

1. **PILLAR 1 — Competence: "I can do this job."**
   Proof you can execute the core function. This is table stakes, not differentiation — everyone in the room can plausibly claim it.

2. **PILLAR 2 — Fit: "I will mesh with this team and this org."**
   Evidence of compatible values, working style, and chemistry (Kelley: often outweighs competence in the actual hiring decision). This is where "why this company" answers live.

3. **PILLAR 3 — Differentiator: "I am not interchangeable with the other candidates."**
   The one thing that makes you memorable and non-generic — traits (Bolles), not skills; REV-filtered strengths (Kelley); a sticky, specific story (LaCivita).

Every answer you give, and every story in your bank, should be tagged to at least one pillar. If you can't tell which pillar an answer is serving, cut it or refocus it — Bolles's data says the best answers run under 2 minutes and can be as short as 20 seconds; padding that doesn't serve a pillar just dilutes your signal.

---

## How to pick your actual 3 messages (not just the 3 categories)

The pillars above are the *slots*. What you put in each slot is specific to you and this job. Use Kelley's **REV filter** to fill them — for each candidate message, ask:

- **R — Relevant.** Does it map to what *this* job posting / this employer's actual pain point needs? (Not a generic virtue.)
- **E — Exceptional.** Does it differentiate you from other qualified candidates, or is it a commodity trait everyone claims ("hard worker," "team player," "integrity")? If everyone says it, it doesn't belong in your 3.
- **V — Verifiable.** Is it backed by a fact, number, story, or third-party evidence (a quote from a boss, an award, a metric) — not just an assertion?

**Process:**
1. Brainstorm ~10 candidate messages (pull from your resume, past performance reviews, and Peggy Klaus's "Take 12" questionnaire in `05-STORY-BANK-TEMPLATE.md`).
2. Score each on R/E/V.
3. Keep the top 3 — one per pillar if possible, though a strong candidate can sometimes cover two pillars at once (a differentiator story that also proves competence).
4. Write each as a single sentence you could say out loud in under 10 seconds. If it takes longer, it's not distilled enough yet.
