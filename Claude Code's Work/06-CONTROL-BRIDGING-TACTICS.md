# Control & Bridging Tactics

Exact phrases for taking control of the conversation, buying time, redirecting away from weak ground, and closing strong. Organized by moment in the interview.

---

## Opening — seize the frame early

**The Preemptive Question** (Kador — the single highest-leverage move for someone who feels underqualified on paper): early in the conversation, ask —
> *"May I ask a question? By what criteria will you select the person for this job?"*

This converts an unstructured, unknowable evaluation into a stated rubric you can visibly map yourself onto, point by point, for the rest of the interview. Take notes as they answer; probe with *"what else?"*

**Deferred-questions positioning** (if you have a lot of your own questions but don't want to fire them early while still being evaluated):
> *"I've got a number of questions I'd love to ask — I want to respect your process first, so I'm happy to hold them until it's a good time."*

---

## Mid-interview — redirecting away from weak ground

**Answer-then-redirect** (after a predictable/exposing question):
> *"[Honest answer] — and actually, that makes me curious: what would you say is [company]'s equivalent challenge right now?"*

**Topic-swap under pressure** (interviewer circling a weak spot): after answering, pivot to a substantive question on different ground. A full detour on a new topic often prevents them from circling back.

**The "shoot it back" move** (for no-win questions like "how do you think this interview is going?" or "what part of this job appeals to you least?"): respond with a clarifying question of your own rather than committing to a risky direct answer — an accepted, expected move when done smoothly, not evasive.

**Answering a hypothetical, then bridging to real proof** (closes the single most common evidentiary gap in interviews — most interviewers ask "what would you do" and never follow up with "what did you do"):
> *"...that's how I'd approach it. Would it help if I gave you an example of a similar situation I actually faced?"*

**Bottom-line self-interrupt** (when you notice yourself rambling):
> *"To cut to the bottom line — ..."* / *"The short version is — ..."*

**Understand-before-you-answer pause:** 2-5 seconds of silence (or restate the question aloud for a complex one) before answering. Reads as thoughtful, prevents "answered the wrong question" mistakes.

**Ask for clarification on a vague question** (sanctioned once, briefly — don't chain multiple):
> *"Happy to answer that — could you clarify which part of [X] you're most interested in?"*

## Handling objections and gaps directly

**ARTS, spoken aloud:**
> *"I understand the concern. Can I ask — what specifically were you hoping to see that this raised a flag on?* [listen] *If I could show you [X], would that put your mind at ease? Let me give you an example — [story]."*

**Surfacing an unstated objection before it kills you silently** (use once real rapport exists, ideally near the close):
> *"Is there anything about my fit for this role that gives you pause, that I could address right now?"*

**Owning a visible gap yourself** (before it becomes a silent disqualifier):
> *"I'll name this directly — I haven't had the exact title of [X], but here's what I have done that's closely adjacent... And I'm someone who ramps fast on new areas — happy to give an example."*

---

## Illegal / invasive questions — the 3-rung ladder

1. **Address the underlying concern, not the literal question** (safest, most common default):
   > *"I sense you might be wondering about [reliability/travel/commitment] — I want to assure you that's not a concern: [evidence]."*
2. **Answer directly, then neutralize** (often most effective — removes doubt entirely):
   > *"Yes, [fact]. If you're wondering about [implication], I've already got that handled — [specifics]."*
3. **Flag it as off-topic, gently** (only if it persists after 1-2):
   > *"I don't think that's directly relevant to the role, but as you can see from my background..."*

## Salary — never name a number first

Escalating ladder (Bolles + Kador, merged):

> *"Until we've both decided this is the right match, I think any salary conversation is a bit premature."*

If pushed:
> *"I'd love to answer that — can you first help me understand what this role involves day to day, so I can give you a number that actually reflects the scope?"*

If pushed again, give a **range**, never a single figure:
> *"Based on my research, I'm seeing a range of $X to $Y for a role like this."*

If they still won't name their own range after that: treat it as a signal their opening figure is likely their final one — decide accordingly, don't keep pushing.

## Closing — install a real follow-up trigger

Run this sequence at the end of every interview so you're never left guessing:

1. *"Based on what we've talked about — can you offer me this job?"* (Direct. Many hires happen simply because the candidate was bold enough to ask.)
2. *"When can I expect to hear from you?"*
3. *"What's the latest I should expect to hear by?"* (Get the worst-case date, not just the optimistic one.)
4. *"May I follow up with you after that date if I haven't heard anything?"* (Installs a legitimate, agreed-upon reason to reach back out.)
5. **If the answer to #1 was no:** *"Is there anyone else you'd suggest I speak to who might be a fit for what I bring?"* — turns even a rejection into a referral.

**Confirm-Assure-Close**, as a closing statement before you stand up:
> *"I want to confirm — you're looking for someone who can [X] and [Y], is that right? [confirm] Based on everything we've discussed, I'm confident I bring exactly that — [1-line recap of your Three Pillars]. [assure] I'm genuinely excited about this role and I'd love to move forward — what's the best next step?"* [close]

## After a rejection — the re-engagement script

Most rejecting interviewers won't give honest feedback out of litigation caution. Remove their fear directly:
> *"I completely understand, and I appreciate you letting me know. If you're open to it, I'd genuinely value your honest read on where I could improve — I won't push back on anything you share, and I won't reach out again for a year. Just trying to get better."*

This also keeps the door open — a rejecting manager is a live network node; the role may reopen if the hire doesn't work out.
