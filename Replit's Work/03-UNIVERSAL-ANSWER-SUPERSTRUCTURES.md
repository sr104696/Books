# 03 — Universal Answer Superstructures

Do not memorize answers to every wording. Identify the question’s job, then
use the smallest structure that supplies evidence and relevance.

## First classifier: “did” or “would?”

**What did you do?** = historical/behavioral. Use a specific SOAR story.  
**What would you do?** = hypothetical/situational. Explain assumptions,
approach, decisions, and expected result.

This is LaCivita’s direct two-type framework; Fry’s behavioral/case
distinction supports it.

## Second classifier: the hidden employer concern

Translate the wording into one or more of Bolles’s five questions:

1. **Why are you here?** Why this employer and role?
2. **What can you do for us?** What skills make you part of the solution?
3. **What kind of person are you?** How will you work with this team?
4. **What distinguishes you?** What makes your contribution or style different?
5. **Can we afford you?** What will it take to hire you?

Answer the future concern beneath a past question, not just the historical
fact. (Bolles)

## Past / behavioral superstructure

**Headline → Situation → Action → Result → relevance → hand-off**

- Headline: name the capability or outcome.
- Situation: give only context and the pain/opportunity.
- Action: emphasize what **you** decided and did.
- Result: quantify impact, or use concrete evidence such as adoption,
  recognition, or a durable change.
- Relevance: “That matters here because [requirement].”
- Hand-off: “Would more detail on [decision/result] be useful?”

Obstacles are optional but useful when they show skillful overcoming. (Kelley)

## Hypothetical / case superstructure

**Clarify objective → state assumptions → sequence approach → explain
tradeoffs → define success → check**

Talk through the reasoning. If the prompt is underspecified, ask one focused
question or state a reasonable assumption before proceeding. (LaCivita,
Fry)

## Opinion / self-description

**Claim → evidence → role fit**

“My approach is [principle]. A time I demonstrated it was [brief SOAR].
That is relevant here because [need].”

## Negative history / weakness

**Fact briefly → ownership → corrective action → positive evidence → future
fit**

Keep the negative portion short, avoid blame or complaint, and spend the
answer on what you did and how the outcome improved. (Fry; Kelley)

## No story comes to mind

Pause. Restate the question in your own words, choose the closest truthful
story by demonstrated skill, and say what is transferable. If none exists,
answer with the hypothetical structure rather than fabricating a past event.

## Safe compression rule

Most answers should be under one minute; a substantial story may take up to
two. If uncertain, state the result early, give the minimum context, and ask
whether more detail is wanted. (Kelley)

## Source distinction

**Direct:** LaCivita’s did/would classifier; Bolles’s five questions; Kelley’s
SOAR and brevity guidance; Fry’s behavioral/case and positive-framing
guidance.  
**Synthesis:** the combined headline/relevance/hand-off formulas.
