# Interview Control Toolkit

Nine source-grounded cheat sheets for presenting value clearly, handling
unexpected questions, and taking an active role in an interview—even when
your experience is not an exact match.

## How to use

1. Extract three to five **REV points** (Relevant, Exceptional, Verifiable).
2. Build one short pitch around them.
3. Prepare story cards, not scripts.
4. Classify every question as **What did you do?** or **What would you do?**
5. Answer the underlying employer concern, bridge to relevant evidence, and
   finish with a useful question.
6. Close by confirming needs, assuring fit, and asking about reservations and
   next steps.

“Underqualified” is not a license to bluff. The source books consistently
favor truthful evidence, transferable capability, learning agility, and a
clear connection to the employer’s needs.

## Source map

- **Thea Kelley, _Get That Job!_** — REV (Relevant, Exceptional, Verifiable)
  points and agenda; REV Intro; SOAR stories; 15-second compression; concise
  answers; talking-points outlines; Company/Role/Boss question organization.
- **Andrew LaCivita, _Interview Intervention_** — the three hiring factors
  (encoding, decoding, memory); the two question types (“did”/“would”);
  Company/Role/Boss buckets; Confirm–Assure–Close; reservations check.
- **Ron Fry, _101 Great Answers to the Toughest Interview Questions_** —
  structured “Tell me about yourself”; behavioral and case distinctions;
  positive handling of negative history; no rote memorization.
- **John Kador, _301 Best Questions to Ask on Your Interview_** — open,
  short, information-rich questions; expectations, objections, and asking
  for the job; questions as a way to steer.
- **Peggy Klaus, _Brag!_** — authentic self-advocacy; 30-second brag bite /
  two-minute bragologue; coherent past–present–future story; specificity and
  audience adaptation.
- **Richard N. Bolles, _What Color Is Your Parachute?_** — five implicit
  employer questions: why here, what can you do, what kind of person,
  what distinguishes you, and can they afford you.

## Direct framework vs. synthesis

Named frameworks above are direct source frameworks. The three-part workflow
**Value → Evidence → Direction** is a synthesis: it operationalizes
LaCivita’s Encoding → Decoding → Memory with Kelley’s REV points, story
evidence, and question/closing tactics. The “bridge” formulas and the
one-page workflow are also synthesis of the cited mechanics, not quotations.

## Limitations

This toolkit uses only the six non-empty extracted text books. Two EPUBs and
one PDF were zero bytes and are unavailable; they were not used. No outside
advice, web material, or invented candidate facts are included.
