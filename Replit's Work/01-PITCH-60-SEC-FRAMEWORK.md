# 01 — 60-Second Pitch

## The job of the pitch

“Tell me about yourself” is a permission to direct attention to **why you
should be hired**, not an invitation to narrate your life. (Kelley, Fry)

## Build the three-point core

Choose **three** (up to five) points that pass all three tests:

- **Relevant:** answers a requirement or likely need in this role.
- **Exceptional:** differentiates you from otherwise qualified candidates.
- **Verifiable:** backed by a result, fact, award, recommendation, artifact,
  or story.

Do not rely on generic labels (“hardworking,” “good communicator”) without
evidence. (Kelley)

## 60-second talking-points outline

**0–10 sec — identity and direction**  
“I’m a [role/domain] who has [one-sentence career summary]. I’m interested
in this role because [specific employer/role need].”

**10–25 sec — REV point 1 + proof**  
“A strength I would bring is [point]. For example, I [action], producing
[result].”

**25–40 sec — REV point 2 + proof**  
“Also, [point]. In [context], I [action], which [result/evidence].”

**40–52 sec — point 3 + transfer**  
“That gives me a useful combination of [capabilities]. It maps to your need
for [requirement] because [truthful connection].”

**52–60 sec — hand-off**  
“Which of those areas would be most useful to explore?” or “How does that
match what you are looking for?”

Use fragments and transitions, not a memorized paragraph. (Kelley, Fry)

## If you need the two-minute version

Add a brief career summary, one more proof, and a small amount of relevant
humanity: a work philosophy, motivation, or interest that supports the
professional brand. End with an open question. Fry’s longer structure is:
introduction → accomplishments → demonstrated strengths → employer value →
development in the role. Kelley targets one to two minutes; Fry gives
roughly 90–120 seconds / 250–350 words.

## Control rules

- Lead with what the employer needs, not your chronology.
- Mention years of experience only when they meet or moderately exceed the
  requirement. (Kelley)
- Give one or two proofs; do not attempt to prove everything in the opener.
- Keep personal information selective and job-relevant. (Kelley)
- Practice aloud with the outline; retain fresh wording.
- If underqualified, name the transferable capability and evidence you do
  have; never invent direct experience.

## Source distinction

**Direct:** Kelley’s REV Intro and talking-points outline; Fry’s five-part
intro; Klaus’s 30-second brag bite / two-minute bragologue.  
**Synthesis:** the timed 10/15/15/12/8-second allocation and the
underqualified transfer sentence.
