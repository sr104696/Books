# 06 — Control & Bridging Tactics

Control means making the conversation clear, relevant, and memorable—not
evading or manipulating it.

## Before the interview: set the agenda

- Write a REV Agenda of three to five points.
- Attach at least two stories to each major point.
- Prepare a 60-second REV Intro and a 15-second version of each key story.
- Prepare employer questions in **Company → Role → Boss** order.
- Bring a short question list; write and practice questions aloud. (Kador)

## In the moment: the bridge

Use this truthful chain:

**Answer the question → name the capability → connect evidence to their need**

Examples:

- “The result was [X]. That demonstrates [capability], which is relevant to
  your need for [requirement].”
- “I have not held that exact title. The closest evidence is [story], where I
  did [transferable action] and achieved [result].”
- “That is one example of how I [REV point]. Would it help to discuss a
  second example?”

These are synthesis formulas based on Kelley’s relevance rule, SOAR, Bolles’s
future-concern test, and Klaus’s audience-aware self-presentation.

## Recovery when surprised

1. Pause; do not fill silence with a ramble.
2. Classify: **did** (story) or **would** (approach).
3. If ambiguous, ask one focused clarifying question.
4. State the structure: “I’ll give the situation, what I did, and the result.”
5. Answer briefly, then ask whether detail is useful.

For an unfamiliar hypothetical, state assumptions and talk through the
approach. (LaCivita; Fry)

## Redirect without dodging

When a question is drifting away from useful evidence:

- “The most relevant example is…”
- “That connects to a similar challenge I handled…”
- “The part most relevant to this role is…”

Answer the legitimate concern first. Then bridge to the strongest truthful
REV point. Kelley says the candidate must educate even an unskilled
interviewer; Kador says questions can steer the interview.

## Negative history

**Acknowledge → action → result → forward bridge**

Keep the explanation factual and brief. Avoid attacking an old employer,
boss, or colleague. (Fry)

## Take control with questions

- Ask about priorities and success measures; use the answer to choose proof.
- Ask open-ended, short, one-point questions. (Kador)
- Invite a concern: “Do you have any reservations about my fulfilling this
  position?” (LaCivita; Kador)
- If there is a misunderstanding, clarify it.
- If experience was not investigated, supply it.
- If a gap is valid, address how your adjacent evidence applies; do not bluff.

## Close: Confirm → Assure → Close

**Confirm:** “I want to confirm that you need [skills] for [responsibilities].
Is that right?”  
**Assure:** “My experience with [evidence] shows I can contribute in those
areas.”  
**Close:** “I am very interested. Is there anything else I can provide, and
what is the next step?”

LaCivita’s direct closing sequence is the most explicit control framework in
the corpus. Reinforce the same REV phrases in the final recap; LaCivita
connects this to later interviewer memory.
