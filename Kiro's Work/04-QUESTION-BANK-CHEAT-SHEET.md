# Question Bank Cheat Sheet

## 8 Meta-Categories That Cover All Interview Questions

Every interview question falls into one of these 8 categories. Master the structure for each category, and you can answer anything.

---

## Category 1: CAPABILITY QUESTIONS
**What they're really asking:** "Can you do this job?"

### Structure: Pillar + Proof
1. State relevant pillar (10 sec)
2. Give SOAR story with results (50 sec)

### Example Questions
- "What are your strengths?"
- "What makes you qualified for this role?"
- "What's your biggest accomplishment?"
- "What technical skills do you have?"

### Sample Answer

**Q: "What are your technical strengths?"**

**A:** "I specialize in building scalable backend systems [Pillar]. At TechCo, when our API started timing out under load, I redesigned it using Redis caching and async processing [A], which reduced response time by 76% and handled 10x the traffic with the same infrastructure [R]. That's the kind of optimization this role needs."

---

## Category 2: COMPATIBILITY QUESTIONS
**What they're really asking:** "Will you fit our culture?"

### Structure: Connect Values + Example
1. Show you understand their culture (10 sec)
2. Give example proving you thrive in it (40 sec)
3. Express enthusiasm (10 sec)

### Example Questions
- "Why do you want to work here?"
- "What kind of work environment do you prefer?"
- "How do you feel about [company value]?"
- "Describe your ideal company culture"

### Sample Answer

**Q: "Why do you want to work here?"**

**A:** "Three reasons. First, your focus on customer obsession aligns with how I work—at my last company, I spent a week shadowing support to understand pain points, which led me to build a feature that reduced tickets by 40%. Second, I thrive in fast-moving environments where you ship quickly and iterate. And third, the technical challenges you're facing with [specific problem] are exactly what I want to be solving. This feels like the right combination of mission, pace, and technical depth."

---

## Category 3: BEHAVIORAL QUESTIONS
**What they're really asking:** "How do you handle situations?"

### Structure: SOAR Story
- Situation (10 sec)
- Obstacles (5 sec, optional)
- Actions (35 sec)
- Results (10 sec)

### Example Questions
- "Tell me about a time you..."
- "Give me an example of..."
- "How have you handled..."
- "Describe a situation where..."

### Sample Answer

**Q: "Tell me about a time you had to convince someone to change their mind."**

**A:** "At DesignCo, our product team wanted to build a complex feature that would take 3 months [S], but I believed a simpler version could deliver 80% of value in 2 weeks [O]. I created a prototype over a weekend, showed it to stakeholders with usage data from similar features, and proposed a phased approach [A]. They agreed to try my approach first. We shipped in 2 weeks, got real user feedback, and ultimately built something better than the original plan—and saved 10 weeks [R]."

---

## Category 4: WEAKNESS/FAILURE QUESTIONS
**What they're really asking:** "Are you self-aware? Do you learn?"

### Structure: Sandwich (Positive-Negative-Positive)
1. Positive frame (growth mindset) (15 sec)
2. Brief honest weakness/failure (20 sec)
3. What you learned & how you've improved (25 sec)

### Example Questions
- "What's your greatest weakness?"
- "Tell me about a time you failed"
- "What would your manager say you need to improve?"
- "What's something you're not good at?"

### Sample Answer

**Q: "What's your biggest weakness?"**

**A:** "I'm constantly working to improve [P], and one area I've developed is delegating. Early in my career, I thought 'doing it myself' was faster and better [N]. But when I became a tech lead, that approach broke down—I became a bottleneck and my team wasn't growing. I learned to trust others, document processes, and give increasingly challenging tasks to teammates. Now I see my job as multiplying impact through others, not doing everything myself. Last quarter, my team shipped 3 major features while I focused on architecture—something that wouldn't have happened with old-me [P]."

---

## Category 5: OBJECTION/RED FLAG QUESTIONS
**What they're really asking:** "Why shouldn't we hire you?"

### Structure: Bridge + Reframe
1. Acknowledge briefly (5 sec)
2. Bridge phrase (5 sec)
3. Reframe to strength (50 sec)

### Example Questions
- "You lack experience in X"
- "This would be a step down for you"
- "You've job-hopped a lot"
- "You're overqualified"
- "There's a gap in your resume"

### Sample Answer

**Q: "You don't have experience managing people. This role requires it."**

**A:** "You're right that I haven't had direct reports [A], but what I have done is lead cross-functional projects with up to 8 people from different teams [B]. For example, I led our migration to microservices, which involved coordinating engineers, PMs, and DevOps. I ran standups, resolved conflicts, kept everyone on schedule, and delivered 2 weeks early. I've also mentored 3 junior engineers who've all been promoted. The skills are there—what's missing is just the formal title, which I'm ready for [R]."

---

## Category 6: MOTIVATION/GOALS QUESTIONS
**What they're really asking:** "Will you stay? Are you ambitious?"

### Structure: Past Evidence + Future Alignment
1. Show pattern from past (20 sec)
2. Connect to this role's opportunity (30 sec)
3. Show ambition balanced with realism (10 sec)

### Example Questions
- "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
- "What are your career goals?"
- "What motivates you?"
- "Why are you leaving your current job?"

### Sample Answer

**Q: "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"**

**A:** "That depends on my performance and the opportunities here, but I can tell you what drives me. I've always sought roles where I can have increasing impact—I started as an IC, then led projects, then mentored others. In 5 years, I see myself taking on progressively larger technical challenges and eventually leading a team. What excites me about this role is that path exists here. You're growing from 50 to 200 people, which means leadership opportunities will open up. I want to prove myself first, but I'm definitely someone who wants more responsibility over time."

---

## Category 7: SITUATIONAL/HYPOTHETICAL QUESTIONS
**What they're really asking:** "How do you think? How would you approach this?"

### Structure: Framework + Example
1. State your approach/framework (20 sec)
2. Walk through steps (30 sec)
3. Reference past example if possible (10 sec)

### Example Questions
- "How would you handle X situation?"
- "What would you do if Y happened?"
- "How do you prioritize when everything is urgent?"
- "How would you approach this problem?"

### Sample Answer

**Q: "How would you handle a situation where you disagree with your manager's technical decision?"**

**A:** "I'd approach it with curiosity first, not confrontation. I'd ask questions to understand their reasoning—there might be context I'm missing about business priorities or constraints. If I still disagree, I'd present my case with data: 'Here's the tradeoff I see... Here's what I recommend... Here are the risks of each approach.' Then I'd defer to their decision since they have broader context. But I'd also ask permission to prove my approach on a small scale if possible. I did this at my last company when we debated database choices—I built a prototype that convinced the team, but I first made sure my manager knew I was exploring alternatives, not being insubordinate."

---

## Category 8: CONTROL/INTELLIGENCE QUESTIONS
**What YOU ask to demonstrate interest, intelligence, and take control**

### Structure: Strategic Question Formula
1. Statement showing research (10 sec)
2. Thoughtful question (open-ended)
3. Listen actively and follow up

### When to Ask
- "Do you have any questions for me?"
- During conversation pauses
- After answering their question (ball in their court)

### Question Categories & Examples

**Expectations Questions:**
- "How will you measure my success in the first 90 days?"
- "What would tell you in a year that this hire was a home run?"
- "What are the top 3 priorities you'd like me to tackle first?"

**Red Flag Questions** (address perceived weaknesses):
- "Do you have any concerns about my background for this role?"
- "What's the weakest part of my experience from your perspective?"
- "Is there anything that would prevent you from recommending me?"

**Company/Team Insight Questions:**
- "What's the biggest challenge your team is facing right now?"
- "How has this role evolved, and where do you see it going?"
- "What do people who succeed here have in common?"

**Direct Close Questions:**
- "Based on our conversation, do you think I'm a good fit?"
- "What are the next steps in your process?"
- "Is there anything I haven't covered that you'd like to know?"

### Sample Question Exchange

**Interviewer:** "Do you have questions for me?"

**You:** "Yes, several. First, I noticed in your recent blog post that you're migrating to microservices [research]. What's been the biggest unexpected challenge with that transition?"

*[Listen to answer, take notes, ask follow-up]*

**You:** "That makes sense. Related to that—how do you see this role contributing to that migration? And what would success look like in the first 6 months?"

*[Shows you're connecting their needs to your role]*

## The Universal Formula

```
EVERY ANSWER SHOULD:
1. Be concise (under 90 seconds)
2. Include "I" not "we"
3. Tie to at least 1 of your 3 Pillars
4. Include quantified results when telling stories
5. End by putting ball back in their court (when appropriate)
```

---

## Quick Category Decision Tree

```
"Tell me about a time..." → Category 3: Behavioral (SOAR)
"What are your [strengths/skills]..." → Category 1: Capability (Pillar + Proof)
"Why do you want..." → Category 2: Compatibility (Values + Example)
"What's your weakness/failure..." → Category 4: Weakness (Sandwich)
"You don't have X..." → Category 5: Objection (Bridge + Reframe)
"Where do you see yourself..." → Category 6: Motivation (Past + Future)
"How would you handle..." → Category 7: Situational (Framework + Example)
"Do you have questions..." → Category 8: Your Control (Strategic Questions)
```

## Preparation Checklist

For each category, prepare:

**Category 1 (Capability):** 
- [ ] 3 Pillars identified
- [ ] 2 proof stories per pillar

**Category 2 (Compatibility):**
- [ ] Research company culture
- [ ] 2 examples showing you thrive in it

**Category 3 (Behavioral):**
- [ ] 20-30 SOAR stories prepared
- [ ] Tagged with skills they demonstrate

**Category 4 (Weakness):**
- [ ] 3 sandwich answers for common weakness questions
- [ ] Never cite weakness critical to job

**Category 5 (Objection):**
- [ ] Identify 3-5 potential objections about your background
- [ ] Bridge + reframe answer for each

**Category 6 (Motivation):**
- [ ] Clear answer on 5-year goals (ambitious but realistic)
- [ ] Authentic reason for leaving current job (positive framing)

**Category 7 (Situational):**
- [ ] Framework for: prioritization, conflict, problem-solving
- [ ] Past example for each framework

**Category 8 (Control):**
- [ ] 10-15 strategic questions written down
- [ ] Prioritized (ask most important first)
- [ ] Include at least 1 red flag question

---

## The 1-Hour Prep Sprint

If you only have 1 hour to prepare for an interview:

**Minutes 0-15:** Identify your 3 Pillars (Category 1)  
**Minutes 15-35:** Write 5 SOAR stories (Category 3)  
**Minutes 35-45:** Write 1 sandwich answer for weakness (Category 4)  
**Minutes 45-55:** Write 5 strategic questions (Category 8)  
**Minutes 55-60:** Practice your 60-second pitch

This covers 80% of what you'll be asked.
